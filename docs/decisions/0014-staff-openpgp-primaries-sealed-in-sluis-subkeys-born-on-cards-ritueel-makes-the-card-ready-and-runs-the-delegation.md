# 0014 — Staff OpenPGP: primaries sealed in sluis, subkeys born on cards; ritueel makes the card ready and runs the delegation

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-11

## Context

[0001](/decisions/0001-scope-roots-and-transitions-systems-like-sluis-hold-intermediates/) leaves company-wide OpenPGP to a record of its own, and [0012](/decisions/0012-personal-openpgp-the-primary-offline-in-a-file-the-daily-subkey-on-a-card/) decides a person's own key. A company that wants its staff to sign commits needs a key it can recognise as its own, stop trusting when the person leaves, and attribute to one person on a hosting platform. This record decides the part of that which belongs to ritueel; the rest belongs to sluis, which holds the primaries. The tracking issue is [#103](https://github.com/truvity/ritueel/issues/103).

The maintainer decided on 2026-11-06:

> **G1 A:** "OpenPGP keys born on cards", with no SSH-signing fallback.
>
> **G2c A:** start with the OpenPGP applet. Add per-purpose PIV keys once a `gpg.program` signer exists.
>
> **G4 A:** keys born on the card, with the attestation recorded in ritueel's ledger; PIN and Admin PIN changed at issue; a second card per person; cards shipped through Yubico Delivery; touch policy decided per key.
>
> **G5 A**, for a leaver: remove them from the org and delete their keys from their GitHub account; let the sluis certification lapse and drop the key from the staff keyring; revoke only on compromise.
>
> **GH1 A:** the keys synced to a person's own GitHub account are only the company-issued ones.
>
> **O1 a** (2026-11-01): the company root delegates, by a domain-scoped trust signature made at a ritueel ceremony, to an online certifier in sluis ([sluis ADR 0024](https://github.com/truvity/sluis/blob/main/docs/decisions/0024-root-custody-and-the-signing-api.md)).
>
> **SK1 E, BK1 A, BK2 A, BK3 A.**
>
> **PX1 yes** (2026-11-06), recorded in sluis ADR 0043 as "the card primary expires after about 3 years and is extended at the card."
>
> **BD1 B** (2026-11-06): "because we manage per-company identities (employees) and comporise of company key is much worse then just persons."
>
> **BD2 B** (2026-11-06): "And even for customers - customers sign/authorize data FOR company to get some service, right?"

G4's "ritueel's ledger" is, under SK1 E, the binding record that sluis keeps; the helper keeps a local record only.

In the maintainer's decisions of the same day, as the author reads them and as sluis ADR 0043 records them: staff keys are **key bindings inside sluis**, and **each person's OpenPGP primary is generated and sealed inside sluis, per company** (BD1 B). A card-born key is a **subkey** under that primary: the person generates it on their own card and binds it with `sluis key bind`, after a fresh hardware step-up, presenting the card's attestation, the card's back-signature over the person's primary, and a signature on sluis's challenge. The first binding of a person is approved by an operator (FB1 A, sluis's). sluis checks all of it on the server, binds the subkeys under the primary, records the binding, and certifies the primary with the company's certification key, which carries the root's trust signature (O1 a). A customer of a company may also bind a key in that company's tenant, for that company only (BD2 B); that is sluis's alone, never certified, never in the staff keyring, and ritueel has no part in it. A key with no attestation can be bound, with the class `unattested` (BK2 A calls it `software`), but company signing for staff refuses it (G1 A). Sealed software keys for non-human identities are a later feature (BK3 A) and nothing more is decided here. sluis produces the staff keyring from its bindings and syncs it to the hosting platform. BK1 A is that ritueel provides the card helper below. PX1 yes is that a card key does expire, after 1095 days (about three years), and the card proves the extension; sluis's primary signs it.

These decisions replace three earlier ones of the same day: a per-person primary held by the company through ritueel in a cloud key service (G2a, G2b), and a primary born on the person's card (SK1 E as first read); the roads not taken say why.

The binding, the record, the primary and its sealing, the certification, the renewal, the keyring and the sync are sluis's, and are decided in [sluis ADR 0043 on key bindings](https://github.com/truvity/sluis/blob/main/docs/decisions/0043-a-person-binds-their-own-keys-to-their-identity-in-sluis.md), accepted and merged on sluis `main` ([pull request #1493](https://github.com/truvity/sluis/pull/1493)). This record states them only as far as ritueel's part cannot be read without them, and **it is the sluis record that decides if the two differ:** the bundle's file names, the challenge's bytes, the notation name, the trust-signature expression, the key identity and the lifetimes are copied from it here, and a change there is a change here.

The roadmap builds the card helper and the delegation after the first signed release.

## Decision

### Staff keys and personal keys are two flows

A **staff key** is an OpenPGP primary that sluis holds, sealed, for one person in one company, with card-born subkeys under it, for the staff address. A **personal key** is a person's own ([0012](/decisions/0012-personal-openpgp-the-primary-offline-in-a-file-the-daily-subkey-on-a-card/), [0017](/decisions/0017-a-persons-openpgp-key-in-two-stages-an-adopted-primary-first-then-card-born-primaries/)), **and that flow is unchanged:** a person's own primary is theirs, in their file, and ritueel's part is the ceremony of 0012. Only a staff key is bound, certified and synced by the company. The OpenPGP applet of a card holds one key in each of its slots, so a card that holds staff subkeys holds no personal key: a person who has both uses two cards.

### What ritueel does and what sluis does

| ritueel | sluis |
|---|---|
| Holds the company roots; runs their ceremonies and transitions ([0002](/decisions/0002-key-custody-every-key-is-born-in-its-store-two-root-cards-or-one-kms-root-and-a-replica/), [0009](/decisions/0009-root-transitions-vouch-overlap-retire-and-revoke/)). | Generates and seals each person's primary, per company, under that company's own data key. The primary signs only the person's own certificate, certify-only, and only for a one-shot grant that `policy` sealed for one use, with packets that key@ builds itself. |

ritueel holds no staff primary, binds no subkey, certifies no person and runs no ceremony per person. The reasons are in the roads not taken.

### The card helper and the proof bundle

- **The helper makes a card ready and produces the bundle (BK1 A).** It checks the firmware floor, changes the PIN and the Admin PIN at the terminal and sets the touch policy per key (G4 A), generates a signing key and an authentication key on the card's OpenPGP applet, reads the card's attestation of both, makes the card's back-signature over the person's primary, and writes the **proof bundle**. It makes no key that is not a subkey candidate: the primary is sluis's. **The handshake is key-first:** the primary's public key and a challenge come from `sluis key bind`; the helper generates on the card, writes the bundle, and a second helper command signs the challenge with the card (below). It has no online dependency on sluis: the files are carried by the person to `sluis key bind`, which reads bundle version 1; the bundle's format, and the test vectors for it, are shared by both repositories.
- **The card keys** are generated on the card as version 4 ECDSA on P-384: a signing subkey (key flag `0x02`) in the signature slot, and, unless it is omitted, an authentication subkey (`0x20`) in the authentication slot, for SSH through `gpg-agent`, as in 0012. **There is no encryption subkey: the helper refuses to make one, and sluis refuses a bundle that holds one** (sluis ADR 0043 says the same, and gives 0012's reason: a lost card would lose the mail it decrypted, and a key the company cannot replace would sit in the company's keyring). No key on the card certifies: the primary is not there; sluis's is ECDSA on P-384 with SHA-384. **The back-signature (type `0x19`) is made by the card with the signing subkey, and by it alone,** over the person's primary and the signing subkey, as OpenPGP requires of a signing subkey, in the form of [the hand-off contract](/reference/hand-off-contract/#the-request-1), with the issuer fingerprint and the issuer key ID. An authentication subkey has no back-signature; **it proves possession by a challenge signature of its own**, because an attestation certificate is public and anyone could present another person's. Whether the authentication slot can make that signature is unverified; if it cannot, no authentication subkey is bound and the bound set is the signing subkey alone.
- **Expiry and extension.** The binding of a card's subkeys expires at most 1095 days after it is made (PX1), as the key expiration time of the binding signature that sluis's primary makes. The card does not make that signature. **The extension is a proof from the card:** the person signs in with a fresh hardware step-up, the helper rebuilds the proof for the same subkeys (`staff proof refresh`), and every subkey of the card signs a challenge with `action: extend`, which carries the stored set and the new expiry; sluis re-verifies the attestations, refuses an `extend` whose set is not the stored one (a wider or narrower set is a `change`), and signs the new binding. A key nobody extends, because the card is lost or the person forgot, ends by itself; sluis warns the person and the operators at 60, 30 and 14 days.
- **P-384 is the contract's rule, and it is kept.** The [hand-off contract](/reference/hand-off-contract/#keys-and-hashes) accepts P-384 only for a subkey, with SHA-384, and the card layer was measured with P-384 on firmware 5.8 ([card libraries](/research/card-libraries-and-tooling-2026-11-01/)). The primary is P-384 too. The contract is not extended for the bundle: the bundle goes from ritueel's helper to sluis, the contract from a system that holds intermediates to ritueel's root, and the bundle is specified on [the reference page](/reference/staff-openpgp/#the-proof-bundle) with its own version, reusing the contract's subkey file and its name, `.subkey.pgp`.
- **Only attested subkeys may appear.** The helper produces exactly two subkey candidates, both generated on the card and both attested, and puts nothing else in the bundle. A subkey from another tool, an encryption subkey, a software subkey or one the card did not attest is never in it; `proof check` and sluis refuse a bundle that holds one (`subkey-not-attested`). Every command that rewrites the bundle re-reads the card and re-checks this first.
- **The firmware floor is 5.7.4,** for staff cards, as the allowlist of sluis ADR 0043 pins it. Firmware 5.7.4 and newer are signed under the maker's new root, "Yubico Attestation Root 1", the only attestation root of staff bindings. Older cards are out: they chain to the earlier root, which a staff binding does not pin. This is stricter than the 5.7.0 of ritueel's other cards ([cards](/reference/cards/#the-attestation-bundle)). The allowlist also names the form factors (the statement carries no model name), and a card whose statement verifies but is not on it gets the class `hardware-unlisted`, which staff company signing refuses: it needs `hardware-attested`. The helper mirrors the floor; the allowlist is sluis's and wins. The OpenPGP applet attests keys since 5.2.3, so the floor is an evidence rule, not the applet's.
- **The attestation rule.** The card attests a key that was generated on it. `ykman openpgp keys attest` takes the slots `sig`, `enc` and `aut` ([Yubico, read 2026-11-06](https://docs.yubico.com/software/yubikey/tools/ykman/OpenPGP_Commands.html)), and the signature and authentication slots are where the two subkeys live, so both can be attested. What the attestation proves is that the key was generated on the device; the key flags are in the binding that sluis's primary makes, and the algorithm in the key. The question of the earlier design, whether the attestation covers a card-born key that certifies, no longer arises, since no key on the card certifies. **Unverified:** that the authentication slot makes a type `0x02` signature; that `gpg --verify` and `sqv` reject a type `0x02` signature as a signature over a document; the Yubico pages read on 2026-11-06 do not state that a P-384 key in either slot is covered, and the encoding of the attestation extensions is not measured ([#18](https://github.com/truvity/ritueel/issues/18)); sluis ADR 0043 records that Yubico says not to trust the fingerprint and date in the statement, so the attested public key is compared with the subkey's. Also unverified: that the signature slot makes the type `0x19` back-signature and the type `0x02` signature through the helper's library, and that `gpg`, `sqv` and the hosting platform accept the result. A conformance test on a real test card settles it before the helper is called built (see Follow-ups).
- **The helper's own checks are a convenience.** It checks the bundle before it writes it (`proof check`), and the attestation chains, the back-signature, the challenge signatures and the firmware again. **sluis does not trust any of it:** it checks the bundle itself, on the server. A bundle from a modified helper, or one built by hand, is as good as its evidence, and the evidence is the cards' attestation chains and the possession proofs, which sluis verifies.
- **The possession proof on the challenge is a domain-separated signature,** in the form sluis ADR 0043 fixes. The challenge is eleven lines of UTF-8 text, each ending in a line feed, with nothing before, between or after, starting with the label `sluis-key-bind-v1` and then `action`, `tenant`, `person`, `boundary`, `key`, `target`, `set`, `expiry`, `nonce` and `not-after`; the exact lines are in [the reference](/reference/staff-openpgp/#the-challenge) and in that ADR, which wins. The proof is an OpenPGP version 4 **standalone signature (type `0x02`)**, one for each subkey, the signing subkey's and the authentication subkey's, each made by that subkey over the same challenge, and all written to `<stem>.possession.sig`. Such a signature is calculated like a signature over a zero-length document and signs only its own subpackets, with the challenge as the value of a **critical** hashed Notation Data subpacket named `bind-challenge@sluis.id`, flagged human-readable. Such a signature parses as no commit, tag, checksum or certification, and a verifier that does not know the critical notation must reject it. A type `0x00` signature over a text would be hashed with the data it follows, and lenient formats tolerate extra lines, so a label inside such a text separates nothing. **What the label does not protect:** the card signs the digest it is handed and does not see the text, so the protection is on the host. The helper refuses a challenge that lacks the label, has other lines, or has a `not-after` in the past; shows the challenge before it asks for the PIN; checks that `key` is the key identity of the card's signing subkey, computed over the exact bytes of [the reference](/reference/staff-openpgp/#the-key-identity); and builds the packet itself. A hostile host or helper can ask the card to sign any digest, and the PIN and the touch are what stand between a card and an unwanted signature. Each card has its own challenge, which lives 600 seconds and is used once. The conformance test checks with `gpg` and `sqv` that the signature parses, and that neither accepts it as a signature over any document.

### Two cards, and a lost card

- **Each person has two cards (G4 A),** and each card holds its own subkeys: a card-born key cannot be copied, so the second card has subkeys of its own, bound under the same primary; one card is one binding, and ending one never ends the other. **A card serves one company (OC1 A):** sluis refuses a card that has an active or pending binding in any tenant, so a person at two companies uses two cards for each. SK2 to SK5 are replaced. Adding the second card needs **two challenges**, because a challenge is signed by the card that its `key` line names: the **old card vouches** (a challenge with `action: vouch`, `key` the old card's signing key and `target` the new one, signed by the old card's signing subkey), and the **new card proves possession** (its own challenge, signed by every subkey of the new card, as for a first bind). The helper is run for each card in turn; an operator's approval replaces the vouching when the person has no active attested binding. A person therefore has one staff primary and four card subkeys, all in the keyring and on the hosting account under one fingerprint. The second card is kept in a second place.
- **A lost card is answered with new subkeys under the same primary.** The person's other card keeps working. The lost card's binding is ended by the person with a proof from the other card (`action: end`) or by an operator; sluis revokes the lost card's subkeys with its primary and drops them from the keyring and the hosting account (G5 A). The person's fingerprint does not change, which is what a card-born primary could not give: attribution on the hosting platform and the identity others have certified survive a lost card. The person gets a new card and runs the helper again, with a new binding.
- **PIN, Admin PIN and touch** are set by the helper at issue and never stored by ritueel (G4 A). **Cards ship through Yubico Delivery;** ritueel does not order, ship or reset cards for anyone.
- **The binding record is sluis's.** Under SK1 E the record of a binding, with the attestation it was made on, is kept in sluis. The helper keeps the bundle and its own local record in the person's directory, and nothing is recorded in a ritueel ledger for a staff binding.

### The delegation ceremony

- **The root makes a trust signature over each company's certification key** (O1 a): one online P-384 key per company behind sluis's key service, which certifies the primaries of that company and no other. The signature is an OpenPGP certification at level 1 (a trusted introducer), amount 120 (complete trust), with a regular expression that limits what the key may certify to user IDs in that company's domain (RFC 9580, sections 5.2.3.21 and 5.2.3.22), marked critical, exportable, and valid for 395 days, like a sluis certification; the validity is the signature's Signature Expiration Time, since a Key Expiration Time in a certification of someone else's key has no effect. The expression, in the text sluis ADR 0043 fixes, is `<[^>@]+@example\.com>$`, with `example.com` the company's domain: the whole address, one `@`, the exact domain, and no subdomain. **An address at a subdomain needs a trust signature of its own.** `sluis key install` refuses a certification key whose trust signature has another expression, none, or a depth above 1. Another company has its own key, its own domain and its own signature. The root stays offline.
- **A separate key is covered by the same ceremony: each company's keyring-signing key.** sluis signs the manifest of the staff keyring with a key of its own, separate from the certification key, so that one compromise gives an attacker either the power to certify or the power to publish, not both. The root makes an ordinary certification, with no trust signature, over the keyring-signing key, valid for 395 days, so that a verifier that holds only the root's fingerprint can check the manifest's signature. ritueel owns the root that can revoke either key, and the delegation ceremony covers both.
- **The hand-off** is the existing one for an intermediate, with a new OpenPGP type `openpgp-certifier`: sluis sends the public key to be certified, a self-signature as proof of possession, a `purpose` (`certify` or `keyring-sign`), the company's domain for `certify`, and the proof file of where the key lives (a cloud key service or a card), and gets back the key with the root's certification. The type is added to [the hand-off contract](/reference/hand-off-contract/) with its checks and vectors in a follow-up; until then nothing in the contract changes.
- **It renews like any intermediate,** at the root's yearly ceremony or under a cloud root's declared policy ([0008](/decisions/0008-the-operator-prepares-the-holder-and-ritueel-creates-attests-and-verifies-the-root/#renewal-of-a-cloud-root-under-a-policy)). That is the one yearly act on the staff path, and it is the root's, not the staff's. A compromised certification key is replaced through ritueel: sluis stops certifying first; ritueel revokes the root's trust signature over it and makes a new one at a ceremony; sluis then re-certifies every primary of that company under the new key. The keyring-signing key is separate, so that compromise does not let an attacker publish a keyring.
- **What each verifier relies on.** A verifier with a web of trust (`gpg` with the root trusted, or `sq-wot`) validates a primary through the root's trust signature and the company key's certification, and rejects an address outside the domain. `gpgv`, `sqv`, apt and rpm do no web of trust: they accept a signature if the signing subkey is in the keyring they are given, bound to a primary that has not expired or been revoked there, and the certification plays no part for them. What they rely on is the keyring and the expiry of the primary and of the subkey. Which verifiers of the matrix evaluate a trust signature with the domain expression is **unverified**, and a follow-up measures it.

### The flows

Who does each step: **ritueel** (the helper or the delegation ceremony), **sluis** (outside this repository), or the **person** or **operator**.

**Joiner**

| # | Step | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The operator orders two cards through Yubico Delivery. | operator |
| 2 | sluis makes and seals the person's primary for the company when the person joins. No approval is needed for this: a primary with no bound subkey signs nothing. | sluis |
| 3 | The person prepares each card with the helper: firmware floor, PIN and Admin PIN, touch policy. | ritueel |
| 4 | The person starts `sluis key bind` after a fresh hardware step-up and gets the primary's public key. On each card the helper generates the signing subkey and the authentication subkey, reads the attestations, makes the signing subkey's back-signature over the primary, and writes the bundle. `sluis key bind` then issues a challenge that names the tenant, the person, the boundary, the card's signing subkey, the set of subkeys and the expiry asked for, and `staff challenge sign` signs it with each subkey on the card. Each card has its own challenge. The person sends the bundle. | person, sluis, ritueel |
| 5 | sluis checks the bundle on the server: the possession signatures, the back-signature, the attestation chains, the one card, the firmware and form factor. It derives the class and records the binding as `pending`. Nothing is bound yet. | sluis |
| 6 | An operator approves the first binding of the person (FB1 A) now that the bundle has arrived, naming the key identities of all subkeys and the card's serial that the bundle carries, and comparing the serial with the company's purchase register. The operator is another person with the `identity-administration` grant, approves with a fresh hardware assertion of their own, and the approval expires within one hour and is used once. For a further card, a vouching signature of an existing card takes the place of the operator. | operator, sluis |
| 7 | `policy` seals a one-shot grant and key@ binds the subkeys under the primary; the binding becomes `active`, and sluis certifies the primary. | sluis |
| 8 | For a `hardware-attested` binding only, sluis puts the person's certificate in the staff keyring, whose manifest it signs with the company's keyring-signing key, and syncs it to the person's own hosting account, once the person has authorised its App. | sluis |

**Mover** (a change of name or address). The primary is sluis's, so the user ID is sluis's to change: sluis certifies the new user ID and revokes the old one from its directory, audits it and notifies the person, and the sync replaces the certificate. The card and the helper are not involved, since nothing on the card changes, and sluis ADR 0043 asks no proof from the card for it. The person has one user ID, the company address the directory holds as primary.

**Yearly.** None for the staff. sluis renews each certification by itself, every 395 days, and the person does nothing. The only yearly act is the root's renewal of the delegation.

**Extension, about every 1095 days.** The person signs in with a fresh hardware step-up; `staff proof refresh` re-reads the card's attestations and rebuilds the proof for the same subkeys, and `staff challenge sign` signs a challenge with `action: extend`. sluis re-verifies, signs the new binding with a later expiry using the primary, records it, and its App uploads the extended certificate again to the hosting account. A person who does not extend, or who has lost the card, loses the subkeys at their expiry.

**Leaver, and a lost card.** sluis ends the binding, which lets the certification lapse, drops the key from the staff keyring and deletes it from the hosting account (only company-issued keys, GH1 A). **No revocation:** old commits stay verified on the hosting platform by design. The key is revoked only on a compromise (G5 A), which the person declares with a fresh hardware step-up of their own, or an operator does when the person has no usable card. A lost card ends only that card's subkeys, as above.

### The security risk, and what controls it

**The risk.** sluis holds the primary of every person. Whoever controls its signer can bind a subkey of their own under any person's primary, which then signs as that person, and whoever controls the company's certification key can certify any key for any address of the company's domain. That is the staff form of the limit [0002](/decisions/0002-key-custody-every-key-is-born-in-its-store-two-root-cards-or-one-kms-root-and-a-replica/#the-accepted-limit) accepts for a root, and it is sluis's to defend: its server checks the attestation and the possession proofs, behind a fresh hardware step-up, which is what the earlier design could not give, since its checks ran on the client. The maintainer weighed this and chose sealed primaries per company and per person (BD1 B, BD2 B): one person's primary is one person's loss, where a key shared by a company is the whole company's.

**What controls it from ritueel's side.** The trust signature is limited to the company's domain by the regular expression, cannot certify beyond one level, expires in 395 days, and is made again only at a ceremony; so a compromised certification key is ended by not renewing it, and at once by a revocation from the root ([0009](/decisions/0009-root-transitions-vouch-overlap-retire-and-revoke/)). Each company has its own, so one company's compromise does not reach another's domain. The helper's checks are not a control against anyone (above). How sluis defends its sealed primaries, its certification key and its records is its own record.

**What stays.** sluis ADR 0043 says it plainly: anyone who controls key@ for a company can make a subkey of their own and bind it under any of that company's people, a compromised `exchange` can obtain a grant for evidence that is false (the one-shot grant, the packets key@ builds itself and its re-check of the signatures stop it from binding any other subkey, not from presenting a forged attestation and approval for a key someone really holds), and one who takes the wrapping key and the ciphertext can do it quietly and offline, with no call to sluis and no audit record; detection is the only control, by the published keyring that anyone can diff and, later, a CI check of commit signers against it. A card with its PIN and touch policy is the person's private key, as for any card. The record states the limit and does not hide it.

### A person's own key

A person's own key is handled by [0012](/decisions/0012-personal-openpgp-the-primary-offline-in-a-file-the-daily-subkey-on-a-card/) and [0017](/decisions/0017-a-persons-openpgp-key-in-two-stages-an-adopted-primary-first-then-card-born-primaries/); a staff key by this record.

### Choices of the author

These are the author's way of filling what the decisions left open, and the maintainer can overrule each:

- **P-384 and the signature and authentication slots for the card subkeys;** no encryption subkey.
- **The 1095 days of PX1 are the expiry of the card subkeys' binding,** as sluis ADR 0043 reads it; PX1 was decided for a card-born primary.
- **BK1 A, BK2 A and BK3 A are read from the maintainer's relayed answers as:** the helper is ritueel's (BK1); a key with no attestation can be bound, in the class sluis calls `unattested`, and staff company signing refuses it (BK2); sealed software keys for non-human identities are a later feature (BK3).
- **The person is present** whenever a subkey is born or a challenge is signed: it needs the card, its PIN and a touch.
- **A staff card is dedicated:** it holds no personal key.

## Consequences

- ritueel runs no service for staff: no per-person key service, no batch ceremony, no audit-trail reconciliation, no staff keyring builder. It keeps the roots, the delegation, the personal flow and a helper.
- ritueel grows the card helper (commands, a proof bundle format with its vectors) and one new hand-off type, `openpgp-certifier`, with the trust signature and its checks.
- The staff path depends on sluis for everything the person experiences: the primary, bind, renewal, the staff keyring and its sync. sluis ADR 0043 is accepted; until sluis and the helper are built, this record's staff path is a target and nothing runs.
- A person's fingerprint survives a lost card and a replaced card, which a card-born primary could not give; the cost is that sluis holds the primary.
- A person holds two cards for each company they work for, and one more if they also keep a personal key.
- The company's trust in a person depends on a trust signature that only a ritueel ceremony can renew, so the root's yearly act includes it, once for each company.
- The conformance test is the gate for calling the helper built: nothing about the attestation of a card-born subkey has been run.
- The extension about every 1095 days is a recurring staff act, and it makes the hosting platform's stored copy stale until sluis uploads the key again. Whether the platform accepts a newer certificate over the stored one, or needs a delete and a create, is unmeasured: its REST documentation lists create, list, get and delete for a GPG key and says nothing about refreshing (read 2026-11-06).

## Roads not taken

- **A card-born primary, bound and certified by sluis (SK1 E as first read).** The private key never leaves the card and sluis holds no key that signs as a person, but the primary is the card's: a lost card changes the person's fingerprint and every attribution and certification with it, the company cannot rotate or recover an identity it manages, and a company that manages per-company identities of employees gets its identity from a device it does not hold; the maintainer chose a sealed primary per person and per company instead (BD1 B, BD2 B).
- **A per-person primary in a cloud key service, held by ritueel's side.** The company could rebind subkeys and a leaver needs no card, but the attestation and presence checks would run in ritueel on the client, so anyone who can call the cloud signer can bind a subkey of their own, it binds the staff path to one cloud provider, and it adds a yearly batch ceremony and a detection pipeline to catch misuse; sluis does the same work with the checks on its server.
- **ritueel holds the staff primaries.** The roots are ritueel's and the staff are not: a command-line tool with a ceremony per person is not a service that binds on demand, and a staff primary held by an offline tool would need a person at ritueel for every joiner and lost card ([0001](/decisions/0001-scope-roots-and-transitions-systems-like-sluis-hold-intermediates/)).
- **One company primary holding everyone's subkeys.** One key to certify and revoke, but a hosting platform ties a key to one account, so attribution to a person breaks; every employee's user ID would sit on one key; and revoking one person would churn the whole key for everyone.
- **An SSH-signing fallback for people without a card.** Easy to start, but it has no OpenPGP identity and nothing the company can certify, and two standards would have to be taught and checked; the maintainer ruled it out (G1).
- **ritueel certifies each person online.** One fewer system for the staff path, but ritueel is a command-line tool and not a network service ([0001](/decisions/0001-scope-roots-and-transitions-systems-like-sluis-hold-intermediates/)), and a root that signs every joiner is not offline.
- **The person's staff key as a subkey under their personal primary.** One identity, but then the company would certify a key it does not control and could not unbind it, and the personal primary is the person's own (0012).
- **Subkeys made in software and imported into the card.** The usual practice, with a backup of the subkeys, but the key existed outside its store and cannot be attested.
- **Ed25519 for the card subkeys.** Native on the applet, but the card layer was measured with P-384, the contract accepts nothing else for a subkey, and the verifier matrix has no Ed25519 subkey on a card.
- **A copy of the subkeys on the second card.** One set of keys per person, but a card-born key cannot be copied without having existed outside its device; the second card has subkeys of its own, as its own binding.
- **A plain document signature (type `0x00`) over a labelled challenge.** The simplest packet, but a label is not domain separation: the same signature form is what a tool takes for a document signature. A standalone signature with the challenge in a critical notation signs nothing else.
- **The keyring signed by the certification key.** One key fewer, but certifying a person and signing a manifest would share a key, and one compromise would give both powers; a dedicated key keeps them apart under one root.
- **Revoking a leaver's key.** Looks final, but the hosting platform never re-checks old commits, so it changes nothing there; it is kept for a compromise.
- **The helper makes an encryption subkey too.** A complete personal-style key, but a lost card would lose the mail it decrypted, which [0017](/decisions/0017-a-persons-openpgp-key-in-two-stages-an-adopted-primary-first-then-card-born-primaries/#day-to-day-cards) avoids for a person's own key only with a software key and a wrapped backup; nothing needs it for staff.
- **The helper talks to sluis.** The challenge and the upload would be automatic, but the helper would depend on a network service and on sluis being up; the bundle is files the person carries.
