<div class="home-intro">

ritueel helps one person or a small team keep a private CA root (the top key of a PKI, a system of certificates) or an OpenPGP primary key for years without losing it, leaking it or letting it expire.

ritueel does not replace your certificate system, your secret store or your cloud key service. It covers what each of them leaves open: the root above them.

</div>

<div class="home-chain">

## The whole chain

The [ceremony](/glossary/) is the one act that uses the root.

<Figure name="whole-chain">
  The design, not a program: a key in a file can be copied without a trace, so it is [replaced](/learn/life-of-a-root/#rotate) on a calendar. A key on a card or in a cloud key service cannot be copied out. So there is no rotation on a calendar: you keep the same key, and the yearly ceremony only [renews](/learn/life-of-a-root/#re-sign) what is due.
</Figure>

The way of working in the picture is in use today, done by hand. The program that guides it is not ready yet: ritueel is in its design phase. What exists is the [design page](/design/), the teaching pages, pictures of [how it works](/how-it-works/) and a [demo](/demo/) of the program's local page, with made-up data. <section>

## Is it for you?

It is for you if you recognise one of these:

- Your CA root or OpenPGP primary key is a file: on a laptop, on a server or in a password manager.
- You renew certificates or subkeys about once a year, and each time you must find out again how you did it.
- A certificate or a key expired, and you learned it when something stopped working.
- You could not say today what your root has signed.
- If the root were lost tomorrow, you would have no tested way back.

Each of these can end in lost days or in a security incident. It is not for you if you look for a service that signs every day. Lost or exposed a key? Read [revoke](/learn/revoke/) first.

</section>
