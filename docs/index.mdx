ritueel helps one person or a small team keep a private CA root (the top key of a PKI, a system of certificates) or an OpenPGP master key for years without losing it, leaking it or letting it expire. A root is the one key that everything else trusts; if it is lost or stolen, everything signed under it is lost or suspect.

The tools are planned: a program that guides you through each use of the root (a [ceremony](/glossary/)) and keeps a record of it.

## What exists today

ritueel is in its design phase. There is a [design](/decisions/), a set of teaching pages and a [demo](/demo/) of the program's local page, with made-up data. There is no program and no release yet. [Where it stands](/status/) says how far each step has got.

## Is it for you?

Yes, if you hold a private CA root or an OpenPGP master key and use it rarely, by hand, with a person present.
No, if you look for a service that signs for you every day.

Nothing here needs a tool today: the pages teach the practice whichever tool you use.

## What the site teaches

- [How a YubiKey holds keys](/learn/how-a-yubikey-holds-keys/): what sits inside the card, what locks it and what wipes it.
- [How passkeys work](/learn/how-passkeys-work/): what a passkey is, how a sign-in goes, and how a passkey can open a key file.
- [How a KMS key works](/learn/how-a-kms-key-works/): what a cloud signing key is made of, and what limits it.
- [Choosing where a root lives](/learn/choosing-where-a-root-lives/): a key file, a pair of root cards or a cloud key, and what each costs a year.

The words are fixed in the [glossary](/glossary/). A first draft of a page on how to verify a release comes with the [release candidate](/roadmap/).

Lost or exposed a key? Read [revoke](/learn/revoke/) first.
