ritueel guides each use of a root key (the top key of your certificates, or your OpenPGP master key) and keeps a record of it. Below: the five ideas, then two real jobs drawn as comics. Jump to [sign an intermediate](#sign-an-intermediate) or [bind the subkeys](#bind-the-subkeys-of-your-card).

ritueel is in its design phase: the program does not exist yet. These pictures show how it is designed to work.

## Five ideas

<Figure name="ideas">

1. [One set of ceremonies](/how-it-works/#every-ceremony-has-six-steps) for a key file, root cards or a cloud key service: the steps are the same, the guarantees are not.
2. [Prefer a key that cannot be copied](/how-it-works/cards/): born on a card or in a cloud key service, not in a file.
3. [One bundle](/how-it-works/bundle-and-stick/) keeps the roots of a PKI and of OpenPGP together, with what is known about the cards.
4. [Requests in, answers out](/how-it-works/#sign-an-intermediate): other systems use files, and a plan made in one place runs in another.
5. [A defined workflow](/how-it-works/#every-ceremony-has-six-steps): ritueel says what is due, and every ceremony has the same six steps.

</Figure>

A requester, such as sluis, needs a certificate for its intermediate key. The root is on a card; a root in a cloud key service would run on your workstation instead.

## Bind the subkeys of your card

ritueel binds the subkeys of your day-to-day card to your own OpenPGP primary, kept in a key file. Each subkey is its own ceremony.

<Figure name="bind-subkeys">
  A passkey on a root card opens the key file: decided, not yet proven.
</Figure>
